How each subject family is taught best. Every lesson follows the general rules; its family adds these.

## maths
Content:
- Start from a situation where the idea is needed (sharing a pizza for fractions, comparing two phone plans for linear equations), then the idea in plain words, then the symbols.
- Concrete, then picture, then symbols: a real situation or objects, then a drawing (number line, area model, bar model, graph), then the equation. Link every symbol back to the picture.
- Give the reason for every rule or formula: where it comes from, shown with a picture or a short derivation, never "just remember it".
- Work one example step by step: each step named by its goal, every operation said, its reason given; then check the answer (put it back in, estimate, look at the units).
- Name the most common mistake at the step where students make it.
- Example and non-example where a definition has a boundary (2 is prime, 9 is not).
Pictures:
- Number lines, area and bar models, arrays, graphs with labelled axes, shapes with their lengths and angles written on them.
- The working written line by line beside the picture, the current line highlighted.

## physics
Content:
- Start from what can be seen or felt (a ball thrown up, a bike braking, a fan slowing down), then the everyday belief about it, then the model that explains it.
- Explain with forces, energy or fields drawn as arrows, then the equation with every symbol said in words and its unit.
- Work the numbers in SI units, step by step, and check that the answer makes physical sense (its direction, its size).
- Name the common misconception and correct it (heavier things fall faster; a moving thing needs a push to keep moving).
- Say the limits of the model when they matter (air resistance ignored, a light string).
Pictures:
- The object with force or velocity arrows that change with the motion; motion along its real path (a ball thrown straight up moves straight up and down, never along an arc).
- Live readouts that change with the motion; the equation built beside the picture.

## chemistry
Content:
- Link three levels explicitly, one at a time: what you see (bubbles, a colour change, heat), what the particles do (atoms, molecules, ions, electrons), and how chemists write it (formulas and equations).
- Use substances learners know (salt, water, baking soda, LPG, rust, lemon juice) and real conditions.
- Balance every equation and count the atoms on both sides; say what each symbol and number means.
- Common misconceptions: breaking a bond releases energy; dissolving is melting; mass disappears in a reaction; atoms of a coloured substance are coloured.
Pictures:
- The real container or object first, then a zoomed-in particle view (atoms as coloured circles marked with their symbols), then the equation; one colour per element in every view.
- Arrows for moving electrons or energy; before and after side by side.

## biology
Content:
- Shape fits job: explain a structure by what it does (thin capillary walls let oxygen pass through).
- A process is stages that follow one thing (one drop of blood, one glucose molecule, one seed).
- Say the scale and move between levels one at a time: organ, tissue, cell, molecule.
- Connect to the learner's own body, food and health.
- Common misconceptions: plants get their food from the soil; we breathe in only oxygen; blood in veins is blue.
Pictures:
- Clean labelled cutaway diagrams, flow arrows, a zoom from organ to cell with the level named.

## computing
Content:
- Run the idea on one small concrete input and show every step: the value of each variable, the state of the list or tree, what is compared, moved or returned.
- Read code aloud in words; keep code to a few short lines and show what each line does to the data.
- Explain cost in plain words before any notation (doubling the list adds just one more step).
- Show one edge case (an empty list, a missing item) and one common mistake (off by one, = versus ==).
- Use something the learner does every day (searching contacts, sending a WhatsApp message, a shopping cart).
Pictures:
- Boxes for variables and array cells with their current values, pointers as arrows, the line of code being run highlighted, a small table of values that fills in step by step.

## networks_systems
Content:
- Before the flow, meet the parts: each component's name and its job in everyday words (a router is like a post office that reads the address and sends the letter the right way).
- Then follow one request, packet or job through the components in order, saying what each one adds, checks or decides, and why.
- Use a real service the learner uses as the request (a UPI payment, a WhatsApp message, a YouTube video, an IRCTC booking).
- Say where it can fail or slow down (a lost packet, a full queue, a server down) and what the system does about it.
Pictures:
- Devices and servers as icons in fixed places, the request or packet moving between them, its headers or fields shown on it as labelled bands that are added and removed.

## economics_business
Content:
- Start from a real money decision in rupees (a kirana shop's prices, a family budget, an EMI, a UPI payment), then the idea, then the general rule.
- Cause and effect as a chain (prices rise, so each rupee buys less, so savings lose value).
- Small round numbers; explain a graph axis by axis before reading it.
- Connect to Indian daily life and institutions (RBI, GST, petrol and onion prices).
Pictures:
- Money moving as arrows between people, shops and banks; bar charts and graphs with labelled axes; before and after with rupee amounts.

## history_society
Content:
- Tell it as a story of people who wanted something: the causes, the event, the consequences, in time order.
- Only the dates that matter, each tied to what happened; cause and effect said with "because" and "so".
- Give different viewpoints fairly where people disagree, and keep facts apart from opinions.
- Connect it to today (a law, a right, a place the learner knows).
Pictures:
- A timeline with the key dates in order, simple icons for people, groups and places, cause-and-effect arrows. No real maps: simple schematic regions instead.

## general
Content:
- The everyday case first, then the idea, then its name; the reason behind it; one concrete example; where it matters in the learner's life.
Pictures:
- One central picture of the case that grows with the explanation.
