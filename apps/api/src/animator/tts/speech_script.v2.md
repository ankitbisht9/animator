For each caption you get its words, numbered, and what the picture shows while it is said. Return how the voice should say it: a list with EXACTLY one entry per caption word, in the same order (entry k is how caption word k is spoken), and its delivery.

## The words (`spoken`)

- In a Hinglish lesson: Hindi words in Devanagari, spelled the way they are pronounced: sunati -> सुनाती, ghumati -> घुमाती, hai -> है, kya -> क्या, dekho -> देखो, samjhe -> समझे, yaani -> यानी, isliye -> इसलिए, tumhari -> तुम्हारी, baari -> बारी, toh -> तो.
- Short Hindi words that look like English are Hindi in a Hindi sentence: in (these) -> इन, un (those) -> उन, to or toh (then) -> तो, me or mein (in) -> में, par (on) -> पर, the (were) -> थे, hi (only) -> ही, ho -> हो, so -> सो.
- English words (science and maths terms, everyday English words) stay exactly as written, in Latin letters: hypotenuse, square, force, energy, plus.
- Numbers stay as digits. A symbol a teacher reads aloud becomes a word: + -> plus, = -> equals, % -> percent, x (a variable) stays x.
- Punctuation is for speaking: keep , . ? ! and turn ; and : into a comma (or a full stop between two complete thoughts); add a comma where a teacher naturally pauses; a question ends with ?. Keep punctuation attached to the entry it follows, and never drop the full stop, question mark or exclamation mark at the end of a sentence.
- Never merge, split, drop or reorder entries. A caption word that is only a symbol nobody reads aloud becomes an empty string.
- When the message says to keep the words exactly as written, copy each caption word unchanged.

## How it is said (the delivery)

Direct it like a real teacher who knows what matters in what they say. Most sentences are said naturally, with no special treatment; only the important parts change. Never exaggerate, and never mark everything.

- `tone`, the feeling of the caption: curious for a question or a puzzle, excited for a discovery, an "aha" or a surprising fact, encouraging for inviting the learner to think or try, thoughtful for a careful explanation or a reason, serious for a warning or a misconception being corrected, warm for a greeting, praise or the recap, neutral otherwise (most captions).
- `pace`: slow for a definition, a formula or equation, numbers being calculated, or the single key idea of the scene; normal otherwise (most captions).
- `stress`: the numbers of the words a teacher stresses: the key term or the word that carries the new idea (often the one marked with **), and the thing the picture reveals or highlights while it is said. One or two per caption, none for a simple transition.
- `pause_before`: the number of the word before which a teacher pauses for a moment so that a key point lands: the term being defined, the answer to the question just asked, the result of a calculation, the key idea. At most one per caption, only at the scene's important moments; usually empty.
